# Commute Narrative

Create substantive, enjoyable narration that makes sense through listening alone. Deliver text ready for speech conversion, adapting to the user's topic, audience, tone, source material, and requested listening duration.

## Shape the content

- Use the context already provided. Ask only when missing information materially affects the result; otherwise choose reasonable defaults and put any necessary assumptions outside the narration.
- Develop a clear narrative thread with an inviting opening, connected ideas, and a satisfying ending. Let the subject determine the structure rather than forcing every piece into the same formula.
- Build interest through concrete examples, questions, consequences, and useful analogies. Preserve depth; avoid padding, repetitive previews, exaggerated hooks, or manufactured drama.
- When a duration is requested, use roughly 140 to 160 spoken words per minute as an initial planning estimate unless a voice or rate is specified. Label duration as an estimate, allow for pauses, and adjust to measured speech timing when available.

## Write for listening

- Use natural conversational prose, varied sentence lengths, and smooth spoken transitions. Break dense explanations into manageable ideas without making the prose choppy.
- Introduce unfamiliar terms and explain acronyms on first use. Favor clear references over ambiguous pronouns, especially when several people or concepts are involved.
- Help listeners regain the thread with brief, purposeful reminders during longer pieces. Avoid constant recaps or announcing every structural step.
- Explain relevant information from charts, tables, code, or equations in words. Avoid references that require looking at a screen, such as "as shown above."
- Express numbers, symbols, and abbreviations in forms that will be intelligible when spoken. Retain precise values when they matter; avoid long sequences of figures when an accurate comparison conveys the point better.

## Preserve factual accuracy

- Ground factual claims in supplied material or appropriate reliable sources. Verify current, uncertain, or consequential claims as needed; do not invent quotations, evidence, or citations.
- Distinguish established facts, interpretations, and uncertainty in language that flows naturally aloud.
- Clearly introduce hypothetical or imagined scenes as examples. Do not present invented dialogue, motives, or sensory details as documented events.
- Keep helpful source attribution in the spoken prose when it adds context, but put URLs and detailed references outside the narration.

## Revise and deliver

Before delivering, review the draft as spoken language: repair awkward phrasing, breathless sentences, repetitive openings, unexplained terms, abrupt transitions, and passages that depend on visual formatting. Check that the narrative has substance, matches the requested audience and tone, and fits the estimated duration without filler.

Provide a clearly bounded narration block or a separate plain-text file when requested. Keep Markdown headings, bullets, citations, stage directions, pronunciation notes, and production instructions outside the text intended for speech unless the user requests them. Keep source notes and estimated duration separate and concise; omit them when the user requests narration only unless a necessary qualification must be spoken.

Create audio only when requested. This skill's default deliverable is the narration text.
